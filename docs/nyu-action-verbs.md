## NYU bullet-writing principles (canonical)

The runAngle SYSTEM prompt encodes these rules verbatim. If you tune the prompt, preserve them:

1. **Focus on the scope of what you did, impact and accomplishments, and quantify results.**
2. **Incorporate a strong action verb to begin each sentence.**
3. **Do not use personal pronouns or write in the first person** (I, Me, We, My).
4. **Describe specific tasks completed and skills demonstrated** from each experience.
5. **Action-oriented descriptive statements** that answer: What did I do? Why did I do it? What was the result? What value did I add?
6. **Skill-based, not task-based.** Not "answered phones" (task), but "identified and troubleshot customer concerns over telephone" (skill).
7. **Highlight transferable skills.**
8. **Quantify or qualify** — use numbers and context for the work, when appropriate.
9. **Consider whether the reader will be able to find value** in the skill described.

## Verb tense

- **Current work**: present tense. (e.g. "Lead", "Organize", "Architect".)
- **Previous work that has ended**: past tense. (e.g. "Led", "Organized", "Architected".)
- Within a single role's bullets, stay in one tense.

## Skills section guidance

- Include **industry, technical, and foreign language** skills.
- Do **not** include soft skills (e.g. "communication").
- Characterize ability with phrases like "familiar with", "knowledge of", "experience in".
- For technical roles, highlight technical skills near the top of the resume.
- For languages, use "(fluent / intermediate / basic)".
